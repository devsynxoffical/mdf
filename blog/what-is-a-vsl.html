What Is a VSL? The Video Sales Letter, Explained

By the Million Dollar Funnel™ team·November 16, 2026·8 min read
Short answerA VSL, or video sales letter, is a sales video that does the job of a written sales page: it grabs attention, explains the problem, presents a solution and makes an offer with a clear call to action. It is usually placed on its own page at the centre of a sales funnel.

VSL meaning

VSL stands for video sales letter. A traditional sales letter is a long piece of persuasive copy; a VSL delivers the same argument as a video, typically a person talking to camera or a sequence of slides with a voice-over. The goal is the same: move the viewer from interest to a single action — apply, book a call or buy.

Because people will often watch what they would not read, a VSL lets a business explain an offer in depth, show proof and build trust before the prospect ever speaks to a salesperson.

How a VSL is structured

Most effective VSLs follow the same broad sequence, adjusted to the offer:

  1. Hook: a specific promise or question that tells the right viewer this video is for them.
  2. Problem: the situation the viewer is in and what it is costing them.
  3. Why the usual fixes fail: the reasons common approaches do not solve it.
  4. Mechanism: the method or system that does solve it, and why it works.
  5. Proof: results, case studies, screenshots or testimonials that make the claim believable.
  6. Offer: exactly what the buyer gets, how it works and what it costs or what happens next.
  7. Call to action: one clear next step, repeated, with a reason to take it now.

How long should a VSL be?

There is no single right length. A simple, lower-priced offer can be sold in a few minutes. A high-ticket coaching, consulting or done-for-you offer often needs a longer video, because the buyer has more questions to answer before they will apply. The practical rule: cover everything the buyer needs to make a decision, cut everything they do not, and use drop-off data to find where viewers lose interest.

VSL vs. written sales page

  • A VSL builds trust faster because the viewer sees and hears a real person.
  • A written page is easier to skim and update, and some buyers prefer to read.
  • Many high-converting pages use both: the VSL above the fold and written proof and FAQs below it.

What is a VSL funnel?

A VSL funnel is the sequence of steps built around the video. A common version for high-ticket offers is: ad or email → VSL page → application or calendar booking → confirmation and reminders → sales call. For lower-priced offers the video page leads to a checkout with an order bump and upsell, followed by a delivery and follow-up sequence.

The video only does part of the work. Viewers who watch but do not act need follow-up by email and SMS, booked calls need reminders so they show up, and every step needs tracking so you can see where people drop off. That is what our done-for-you VSL funnels include — and how one offer reached $1.52M in 19 months.

Metrics that show whether a VSL is working

  • Play rate: the share of page visitors who start the video.
  • Retention: where in the video viewers stop watching.
  • Call-to-action clicks: how many viewers reach and click the next step.
  • Application or checkout completion rate.
  • Show-up rate and close rate for booked calls.

Improving the weakest of these numbers is usually cheaper than buying more traffic. A strong hook raises play rate and retention; a clearer offer raises clicks; reminders raise show-up rates. Where each number sits in the journey is covered in our guide to sales funnel stages.

When a VSL is the right choice

Use a VSL when buyers need to understand something before they will buy: a new method, a premium price or a service they have not bought before. If your sales calls all start with the same explanation, that explanation probably belongs in a VSL. If your audience responds better to a scheduled event, consider a webinar funnel instead. If your offer is simple and price-driven, a short landing page will usually convert as well for far less effort.

Frequently asked questions

What does VSL stand for?+

VSL stands for video sales letter: a sales video that presents an offer and asks the viewer to take one action, such as booking a call or buying.

Is a VSL the same as a webinar?+

No. A webinar is usually longer, often live or scheduled, and teaches before it sells. A VSL is a focused, on-demand sales video placed on a single page.

Do VSLs still work?+

Yes, when the offer needs explaining. They work best as one part of a funnel with follow-up, not as a standalone video.
